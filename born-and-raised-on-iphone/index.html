Born and Raised on iPhone

2010–2026years of work16iPhone generations59photographs

“If you can do this with an iPhone, imagine what you could do with a real camera.”

Eran Yerushalmi, known as CHIEF, heard that sentence again and again. He never switched to the “real” camera. He kept shooting on iPhone: seventeen years, sixteen generations of iPhone, roughly 30,000 photographs. Since 2009 all of his work has been shot and edited on the phone.

It started at home. In 2010 he photographed his older daughter Mia asleep on the sofa, and her reflection in a mirror. Tiny files, a few hundred pixels across. The eye was already there.

From there he went out to the shore and the streets of Tel Aviv. He works close to the ground, at the edge of the water and inside it, waiting for the moment when a puddle turns a street upside down or the low sun turns a passer-by into a silhouette. Over the years Emma, his younger daughter, and Dylan, the family dog, joined the frame.

The work has reached exhibitions, large-format prints, Channel 13, Yedioth Ahronoth, Maariv and the Tel Aviv–Yafo Municipality’s channels.

So what would have happened with a real camera? This page is the answer.

One iPhone at a time, from a 2–3 megapixel phone in 2010 to the 48 megapixel iPhone 16 Pro Max. Every photograph is shown in its full original frame, under the model it was shot on, with the size of the original file. The camera changes on every page. The eye does not.

Chapter three

The street as a mirror

Rain leaves water on the pavement and he gets down to it. Buildings, palms and people appear twice, once standing and once reflected. Reflection becomes his signature. Then a new figure enters the frame: his younger daughter Emma, leaping on the shore and walking upside down through the sky of a puddle. On the shore another member of the family joins in: Dylan · part of the family, who has an Instagram account of his own, @dylan_tlv.

Why this story

Seventeen years. One device. One eye.

Most iPhone photography stories show what one new phone can do. This archive shows something rarer: what sixteen generations of iPhone made possible in the hands of the same artist, in the same city, from the first photograph of Mia to his current work.

Every file is labelled with the model it was shot on. Every image is shown uncropped. The progression is visible in the pixels, and so is everything that did not change.

CHIEF is available for an artist feature or interview, and can provide full-resolution files, the device-by-year overview, and press and exhibition material for the selection agreed.
